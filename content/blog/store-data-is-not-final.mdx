A dashboard that reads store APIs literally will show you things that did not happen. Not because
the APIs are wrong, but because they have conventions that are easy to miss and expensive to get
wrong.

## Recent days get restated

Apple and Google both revise the last few days as attribution settles and fraud filtering runs.
Yesterday's install count is a draft. It usually moves by a small percentage; occasionally more.

Two consequences:

**Do not alert on yesterday alone.** A threshold rule reading a not-yet-settled figure will fire on
a number that corrects itself by lunchtime. Evaluate over a window.

**Re-fetch a trailing range on every sync.** Pulling only new days means the restatement never
lands and your history quietly diverges from the console. Re-pull the last 7 to 30 days and upsert.

## A missing day is not a zero day

The most visible version of this bug: a dashboard where every chart dives to zero each morning.

If today has not synced yet and your zero-fill runs to the current date, you have drawn a
catastrophic crash that is actually just "the job has not run". Users see it, panic, and lose trust
in the chart permanently.

Interior gaps are different — a missing day between two populated days genuinely is a zero, or at
least is safe to draw as one. It is the *trailing* edge that needs trimming to the last day you
actually have data for.

## Dates are not timezones

Store APIs report `2026-08-01` meaning that calendar day in the report's own timezone. Parse that
string with a naive date constructor and, on any server east of UTC, you get local midnight — which
converts to July 31st.

Every metric shifts one day earlier. The chart looks completely normal. The totals are right. The
individual days are wrong, and it will only show up when someone compares a specific day to the
console and finds it matches yesterday.

Parse date-only strings as UTC explicitly. This is a two-line fix that is almost impossible to spot
after the fact, because nothing about the output looks broken.

## Zero rows are omitted

Ad platforms generally do not send a row for a day with no spend. That absence means "no spend",
not "no data" — but only inside the span the platform was actually reporting. Before your account
existed, the same absence means nothing at all.

If you are deriving anything from that data, the distinction decides whether a gap is a zero or an
unknown. Treating a pre-account gap as a zero-spend day will make historical organic figures look
better than they were.

## Different definitions of the same word

- Firebase counts **first opens**. Play Console counts **installs**. Related, not equal.
- Apple's Search Ads "installs" includes **redownloads**, which is why it can exceed new downloads.
- "Impressions" from an ad network means ad impressions. "Impressions" from Play Console means
  store listing views. Same word, unrelated measurements.

Any system that lets those share a metric key without recording which provider it came from will
eventually add two of them together, and the result will look plausible.

## The rule that covers all of it

Record the source with every number. When two providers disagree — and they will — the question
"which one is this?" needs an answer that does not involve reading the sync code.
