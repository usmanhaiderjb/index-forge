Most teams treat localisation as a product project — translate the app, then translate the
store listing. That ordering leaves the cheapest ranking work in ASO untouched for years.

**You can localise metadata without localising the app.** Users in a storefront search in
their own language, find your listing in their own language, and install an app whose
interface is in English. Whether that is a good experience depends on the app, but the
ranking effect is immediate and the cost is a translator's afternoon.

There is also a mechanical trick on iOS that most people miss entirely.

## The iOS locale trick

Apple indexes your keyword field **per localisation**. Add a localisation, and you get
another 100 characters of keyword field for that storefront.

More usefully: **some storefronts search across more than one locale's metadata.**

The most-cited case is the US App Store, which indexes both `English (U.S.)` and
`Spanish (Mexico)` metadata for users in the United States. Adding Spanish (Mexico)
localisation gives you an extra 100 keyword characters that US users can match against —
without changing what an English-speaking user sees.

Similar pairings exist elsewhere. The mechanism is worth checking for your priority
storefronts, because the effect is a straight expansion of your keyword budget in the
market you already care about.

**A caution:** Apple's locale indexing behaviour is not documented as a contract and has
changed before. Verify it for your storefronts rather than taking any blog post — including
this one — as a specification. Add the localisation, track the terms, and see whether they
rank.

## Translated keywords are usually wrong keywords

This is where most localisation work goes astray.

You cannot translate a keyword list. You have to **research the target market's keywords in
that market's language**, because:

- **People search differently in different languages.** The literal translation of "habit
  tracker" may not be what German users type. They may use an English loanword. They may use
  a phrase with no English equivalent.
- **English loanwords are common in some categories and absent in others.** In many markets
  "fitness" is searched in English while the rest of the phrase is local.
- **Search volume distributes differently.** A term that is head in one market is long tail
  in another.

The process is the same as any keyword research, run again with the storefront set to the
target market:

1. Set your device or tool to the target storefront.
2. Expand seeds through **that storefront's** autocomplete.
3. Check what actually ranks there — often not the apps you expect.
4. If you run ASA, segment the search-term report by storefront. This is the highest-quality
   answer available, and it is per-market. See [Apple Search Ads is the only real keyword
   volume data you can get](/blog/apple-search-ads-keyword-data).

## Which markets first

Rank your storefronts by **installs you already get**, not by market size in the abstract.

A market already sending you organic installs with no localised metadata is telling you
there is unserved demand. That is a better bet than a large market you have no presence in.

Then weight by:

- **Language reach.** Spanish and Portuguese cover many storefronts each; Japanese covers
  one.
- **Competitive density.** Smaller markets are frequently less contested, so the same work
  buys more rank.
- **Revenue per user**, if monetisation differs sharply by market.

## What to localise, in order

1. **Keyword field (iOS)** — pure ranking, invisible to users, no design work. Cheapest
   possible win.
2. **Title and subtitle** — ranking plus conversion. Watch length: German and Finnish
   routinely overflow 30 characters, and truncation is worse than English.
3. **Screenshot captions** — the highest-conversion localisation, and consistently
   underrated. Translated captions on English UI screenshots still beat English captions.
4. **Short description (Android)** — indexed and read.
5. **Full description (Android)** — indexed in full, so it carries real ranking weight.
6. **The app itself** — the expensive one, and the one to do last unless the app is
   unusable without it.

Note that items 1 through 5 are all metadata. None require a build.

## The Play difference

Google Play has no separate keyword field, so the localisation win is smaller and comes
through the description instead — which is indexed in full, in each locale.

Play also offers **automatic translation** of your listing. It is better than nothing for
reach and worse than a translator for ranking, because it translates literally and literal
translations are frequently not what people search. Treat it as a placeholder, not a
strategy.

## Measuring it

Localised metadata is one of the few ASO changes with a clean measurement, because the
effect is confined to a storefront.

- Track rank **per storefront**, not averaged. An average across countries hides that you
  climbed in Mexico and did nothing in Spain.
- Watch **conversion rate per storefront** — localised screenshots move this fastest.
- Give it weeks. Re-indexing is not instant, and low-volume storefronts are noisy.

An average rank across markets is one of the least informative numbers in ASO. It moves
when your traffic mix moves, even if nothing changed anywhere.

## The short version

- Metadata localisation does not require app localisation.
- On iOS, extra localisations can expand your indexed keyword budget in markets you already
  serve — verify the behaviour for your storefronts rather than assuming it.
- Never translate a keyword list. Research the market in its own language.
- Prioritise markets already sending you organic installs.
- Keyword field first, screenshots close behind, the app itself last.
- Track rank and conversion per storefront; averages hide everything.
