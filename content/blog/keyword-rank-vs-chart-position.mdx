Two numbers get called "rank" and they are not the same measurement.

**Keyword rank** is where your app appears when someone searches a term. It moves on relevance:
metadata, conversion rate for that term, ratings, engagement.

**Chart position** is where you appear in Top Free, Top Paid or Top Grossing for a category and
country. It moves on *velocity* — downloads in a recent window, weighted toward the last few days.

A campaign burst lifts chart position within a day and does almost nothing to keyword rank. A
metadata change lifts keyword rank over a week or two and does nothing to chart position until the
extra installs arrive. Confuse them and you will respond to a chart drop by rewriting your
subtitle, which is roughly like responding to a flat tyre by changing the oil.

## Null rank is not rank 100

Rank tracking scans the top N results. If your app is not in them, the honest record is "not found
in the top N", not "rank N+1" and not zero.

This matters more than it sounds. Three failure modes come out of getting it wrong:

**Zero-filling.** Plot a missing rank as 0 and, on an axis where 1 is best, you have drawn your
app as the best result in the world on the day it fell out of the results entirely.

**Missing rows.** Skip the write instead, and "we did not rank that day" becomes indistinguishable
from "the scan did not run". Those need opposite responses.

**Unrecorded scan depth.** Store the null but not how deep you looked, and the number is
uninterpretable a month later. "Not in the top 10" and "not in the top 100" are very different
facts. Worse, if someone changes the scan limit, every historical null silently changes meaning.

Record the null *and* the depth alongside it. Then a gap on the chart is a real, readable event.

## Track competitors on the same page

When you scan a keyword's results you already have the whole result page in memory. Reading only
your own position and discarding the rest throws away the more useful half of the data.

Your rank moving from #12 to #15 means one thing if everyone moved down — someone new entered the
category — and something else entirely if your competitors held position. The first is a market
event. The second is about you.

Capturing competitor positions from the scan you already ran costs nothing: no extra requests, and
critically, it is the *same* result page your own rank was measured on. Fetching competitor ranks
separately later gives you positions from a different page at a different moment, which is not
comparable and quietly is not.

One detail: record an explicit null when a tracked competitor is absent, rather than writing no
row. A competitor dropping out of the top 100 is a finding worth seeing, and a missing row looks
identical to a scan that never happened.

## What to watch

- **Keyword rank** for whether your listing is getting more relevant.
- **Chart position** for whether download velocity is holding.
- **Both, together with your competitors**, for whether a move was about you or about the market.

The two numbers answer different questions. Reporting them under one word is how teams end up
optimising the wrong surface for a month.
