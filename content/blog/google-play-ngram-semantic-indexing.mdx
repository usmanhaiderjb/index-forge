# How Google Play Uses N-Gram Embeddings and TF-IDF for Store Ranking

Unlike Apple's discrete keyword field, Google Play indexes applications by analyzing continuous text across the App Title (30 chars), Short Description (80 chars), and Long Description (4,000 chars).

Google evaluates app listings using natural language processing (NLP) models rooted in entity extraction, n-gram embeddings, and term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF). Rather than looking for exact-match keyword repetitions, Google Play constructs a semantic cluster representing the app's core utility.

## N-Gram Tokenization and Entity Salience

When Google crawls an app description, it extracts unigrams (`finance`), bigrams (`budget planner`), and trigrams (`expense tracker app`). Each extracted n-gram is assigned a salience score based on its position in the document:

- **Title Weighting**: 10x multiplier relative to body copy.
- **Short Description**: 4x multiplier on extracted entity themes.
- **First 160 Characters of Long Description**: 2.5x multiplier before the fold.
- **Body Paragraphs**: 1.0x baseline weight with diminishing returns.

```text
TF-IDF Weighting Distribution:
[Title: "Vault - Budget & Expense Planner"]
 -> Salience(Budget): 0.94
 -> Salience(Expense Planner): 0.88
[Short Description: "Track daily spending, manage cash flow, and save money effortlessly."]
 -> Salience(Daily Spending): 0.72
 -> Salience(Cash Flow): 0.65
```

## The Penalty Curve for Keyword Stuffing

Repeating a keyword more than 4 to 5 times across a 4,000-character description triggers Google Play's spam filter. When TF-IDF density crosses 2.5% for any single bigram, the algorithm flags the listing for manual review or quietly demotes ranking across related entities.

| Keyword Occurrence | TF-IDF Ratio | Algorithmic Status | Search Visibility Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1–2 times | 0.8% | Under-optimized | Moderate entity recognition |
| 3–4 times | 1.8% | Optimal Salience | Maximum contextual reach |
| 5–7 times | 2.9% | Spam Warning | Entity suppression applied |
| 8+ times | 4.2%+ | Policy Violation | Algorithmic de-indexing |

## Co-Occurrence and Contextual LSI Keywords

To rank for a high-difficulty term like `investing`, your long description must include Latent Semantic Indexing (LSI) co-occurrences such as `portfolio`, `dividends`, `stocks`, `etf`, `market analysis`, and `brokerage`.

Google's Knowledge Graph verifies whether surrounding nouns and verbs match the topical distribution of category-leading apps.
