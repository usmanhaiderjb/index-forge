"Our ranking dropped" is one of the least useful sentences in app marketing, because it
describes at least three different events with three different causes and three different
responses.

This guide separates them: what a keyword rank is, what a chart position is, what actually
moves each, and how to tell a real change from an artefact of how you measured.

## Two numbers, both called "rank"

**Keyword rank** is where your app appears when someone searches a specific term. It
answers: *for the phrase "habit tracker", are we third or thirtieth?*

**Chart position** is where you appear in Top Free, Top Paid or Top Grossing for a
category or country. It answers: *among all fitness apps, how are we doing right now?*

They move for different reasons and on different timescales.

| | Keyword rank | Chart position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Driven by | relevance and conversion for that term | download velocity |
| Timescale | days to weeks | hours to days |
| Responds to | metadata, ratings, engagement | a spike in installs |
| Fixed by | better targeting and listing | more downloads, faster |

A drop in chart position with stable keyword ranks means your download velocity fell —
possibly because a campaign ended, possibly because a competitor's campaign started. A
drop in keyword rank with a stable chart position means a relevance change: your metadata,
your conversion rate for that term, or a competitor's.

**Reading one as the other sends you optimising the wrong thing.** Rewriting your subtitle
will not fix a velocity problem, and buying installs will not fix a relevance problem.

## What moves a keyword rank

Both stores keep their algorithms private. What follows is the consensus of what is
observable, not a leaked spec.

### Relevance signals

- **Metadata match.** Whether the term appears in the fields the store indexes — and as
  covered in [our keyword research guide](/blog/app-store-keyword-research), those fields
  differ sharply between iOS and Android.
- **Field weight.** Title outweighs subtitle, which outweighs the keyword field. A term in
  your title is a stronger claim than the same term buried in keywords.
- **Exact versus combination match.** Apple builds combinations across your fields, so
  "habit" and "tracker" in separate fields can rank for "habit tracker".

### Performance signals

- **Tap-through rate** for that term. If people search a keyword, see your app, and scroll
  past, the store learns you are not the answer.
- **Conversion rate** from product page to install.
- **Retention and engagement** after install. Both stores factor in whether users keep the
  app. This is the mechanism that punishes ranking for terms you do not serve.
- **Ratings and review velocity.** Covered in the [reviews
  cluster](/blog/category/reviews) — the effect is real but slower than most people expect.

The performance signals are why keyword targeting cannot be gamed for long. Ranking for a
term you do not serve produces installs that churn, and churn feeds back into ranking.

## What moves a chart position

Charts are much simpler and much more brutal: **download velocity, weighted heavily toward
the last few hours.**

A few consequences follow:

- Charts move fast. A campaign can lift you within a day and drop you within a day of
  stopping.
- Charts are not a measure of ASO. They are a measure of acquisition.
- Category charts are winnable in a way overall charts are not. Few apps ever appear in an
  overall top 200; a category chart is a realistic target.

If your chart position is your headline metric, you are measuring your paid spend.

## The measurement problems

This is where most ranking reports quietly lie, and none of it involves the stores doing
anything wrong.

### Scan depth

Rank tracking works by searching a term and finding your app in the results. But the search
only goes so deep — typically the top 100 or 200.

If your app is not in that window, the honest answer is **"not found in the top 100"**.
What most tools record is a blank, or worse, a fixed number like 250.

Those are different claims. "Not in the top 100" and "ranked 250th" look identical in a
chart and mean different things: the first is a limit of the measurement, the second is a
measurement. **A rank report that does not record its own scan depth cannot be
interpreted** — you cannot tell a genuine fall from an app that simply moved past the edge
of the scan.

### Personalisation and location

Search results differ by country, language, device, and sometimes by user history. A rank
measured from one location is not a global truth. If your tool does not tell you which
storefront a number came from, it is telling you a number from *somewhere*.

### Timing

Rank is a snapshot. Measured at different times of day, a term that sits at the boundary
between positions 10 and 11 will look like it is oscillating. It is not; your sampling is.

The fix is consistency: same time, same storefront, every day. An inconsistent measurement
is worse than a less frequent one.

### Competitor comparisons taken separately

If you measure your own rank now and your competitor's in ten minutes, the two numbers are
not comparable — the result page may have changed between them.

Competitor positions are only meaningful when **captured from the same result page your own
rank was measured on.** Anything else is comparing two different moments and calling it a
gap.

## How to read a rank chart properly

1. **Check the scan depth first.** If it is not recorded, distrust every gap in the series.
2. **Look at the trend, not the day.** Daily movement of a few positions is noise on almost
   any term.
3. **Segment by storefront.** An average across countries hides the fact that you are
   climbing in one market and falling in another.
4. **Correlate with what you changed.** If you cannot point at a metadata change, a rating
   shift, or a campaign, you are probably looking at noise.
5. **Check whether the term itself moved.** Terms rise and fall in popularity. Holding
   position on a term nobody searches any more is not a win.

## What to do when a rank drops

Work through it in this order, because the cheap checks eliminate most cases:

1. **Is it a measurement artefact?** Scan depth, storefront, timing. Most "drops" die here.
2. **Did the chart move too?** If both fell together, it is velocity, not relevance.
3. **Did you change metadata recently?** Both stores re-index on a delay. A dip in the days
   after a change is common and often recovers.
4. **Did your ratings move?** A drop below a psychological threshold — 4.0 especially —
   affects both ranking and tap-through.
5. **Did a competitor change?** Someone else's new subtitle can push you down a term
   without anything on your side changing at all.
6. **Is the term seasonal?** Some categories swing hard by month.

Only after all six is "our ASO got worse" the likeliest explanation.

## The short version

- Keyword rank measures relevance. Chart position measures velocity. Do not treat one as
  evidence about the other.
- Post-install engagement feeds ranking, which is why targeting irrelevant terms
  backfires.
- **Scan depth is the single most important field in a rank report** and the one most often
  missing.
- Competitor positions are only comparable when captured from the same result page.
- Most drops are measurement artefacts, delayed re-indexing, or someone else's change.
