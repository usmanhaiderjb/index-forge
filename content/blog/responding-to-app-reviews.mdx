Replying to reviews is the highest-leverage reputation work available, and the mechanics differ
enough between stores that a single implementation gets one of them wrong.

## The limits are not the same

Google Play caps developer replies at **350 characters**. Apple allows close to **6000**.

That is a seventeen-fold difference, and a UI that shows one limit for both is actively
misleading. Showing an iOS reply "342 / 350" characters while Apple would happily accept sixteen
times that will produce cramped, unhelpful replies for no reason. Showing Android "1200 / 5970"
lets someone write a reply that will be rejected on submit.

Each store gets its own limit, and where a store's limit is not known, fall back to the more
conservative one rather than guessing high.

## Editing works differently

On Google Play you can edit a reply after publishing. On the App Store you can update a response,
but the change is user-visible and Apple may re-review it.

The practical rule: treat every reply as permanent when you write it, and rely on editing only as
damage control.

## The failure mode nobody plans for

Publishing a reply is a write to a public, user-visible surface. Which makes it exactly the kind of
operation that must not be retried blindly.

A request times out. The reply may have been published; it may not. A generic retry wrapper — the
same one that is entirely correct for a metrics fetch — now publishes a second reply, or overwrites
a first one, on a public review page. Users see it. It cannot be quietly rolled back.

Read-only calls retry freely. Publishing calls fail loudly on an ambiguous response and let a human
decide. If a reply implementation shares its retry policy with the metrics fetcher, that is a bug
waiting for a slow day at the store API.

## Themes beat averages

An average rating that moves from 4.4 to 4.2 tells you something is wrong and nothing about what.
The reviews themselves say "crashes on open after the last update", but only if you read all of
them, every day.

Classifying reviews into themes turns the rating drop into a specific report — a spike in reviews
tagged `crash` and `onboarding` in the days after a release. That is a bug ticket, not a metric.

Alert on the themes as well as the number. A rating average moves slowly by construction: by the
time it has fallen far enough to trip a threshold, the cause is weeks old and the reviews
describing it have scrolled away.

## What to actually reply to

In order:

1. **One and two-star reviews describing a specific, fixed bug.** The highest-value reply there
   is: users often update their rating.
2. **Reviews asking a question with an answer.** Public, so the next person with the same question
   finds it.
3. **Detailed positive reviews.** Cheap, and it signals an active developer to everyone reading.

Skip the generic thank-you on every five-star review. It reads as automated, because it is.
