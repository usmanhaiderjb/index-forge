A store listing does two jobs at once, and they pull in different directions. It has to be
**found**, which is a keyword problem, and it has to **convert**, which is a persuasion
problem. Fields that are indexed are not always the fields that sell, and optimising every
field for search produces a listing that ranks and nobody installs.

This guide goes field by field: what each one is indexed for, what it converts, and where
the limits are.

## The fields, and what each one is for

| Field | iOS | Android | Indexed | Converts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| App name / title | 30 chars | 30 chars | **heavily** | yes |
| Subtitle | 30 chars | — | **yes** | yes |
| Short description | — | 80 chars | **yes** | yes |
| Keyword field | 100 chars | — | **yes** | no (invisible) |
| Description | 4,000 chars | 4,000 chars | **iOS: no** · Android: yes | some |
| Icon | — | — | no | **heavily** |
| Screenshots | 10 | 8 | no | **heavily** |
| Preview video | 3 | 1 | no | yes |
| Promotional text | 170 chars | — | no | yes |

Two rows deserve emphasis because they are the most common mistakes:

**The App Store does not index your description.** Writing it for keywords costs you
nothing in ranking and everything in conversion. Write it for a human.

**Icons and screenshots are not indexed at all** and are probably the highest-leverage
things on the page. Most people decide from the search results grid, having read your icon,
your name, and possibly your first screenshot.

## Title and name

The highest-weighted indexed field on both stores, and the only text guaranteed to be read.

Thirty characters is brutally short. The usual shape is:

> **Brand: primary keyword**

`Pocketwise: Budget Planner` — brand for recall, keyword for discovery.

A few rules:

- **Never repeat a word between title and other indexed fields on iOS.** Apple indexes the
  union of your fields, so a repeat is wasted characters.
- **Watch the display truncation.** Your full 30 characters appear on the product page, but
  search results and the home screen truncate far shorter — often around 12 to 18
  characters depending on device. Put the important part first.
- **Do not stuff.** Both stores reject or penalise titles that are keyword lists, and a
  title that reads like spam converts badly even when it ranks.

## Subtitle and short description

iOS gives you a 30-character subtitle. Play gives you an 80-character short description.
Both are indexed, and both are read.

This is where the *second* head term goes, and where you say what the app does in plain
language. The pattern that works is a benefit plus a qualifier:

> `Track habits, build streaks`

Play's 80 characters are enough for a full sentence, and it appears prominently near the
top of the listing. Do not waste it restating your title.

## The iOS keyword field

One hundred invisible characters, and the most misunderstood field in ASO.

- **Comma-separated, no spaces after commas.** `habit,tracker,routine` — a space costs you a
  character for nothing.
- **No repeats** of any word already in your title or subtitle.
- **No plurals** where you already have the singular. Apple handles basic stemming.
- **Skip your category name.** You are already indexed for it.
- **Skip competitor brand names.** Apple removes them and it can escalate to a legal
  problem.
- **Singles, not phrases.** Apple builds combinations across your fields, so `habit` and
  `tracker` gives you "habit tracker" for free. Spending characters on the phrase wastes
  the space.

## The description

Here the stores diverge completely.

**Google Play indexes the full 4,000 characters.** Your secondary keywords belong here,
used naturally, two or three times each. Over-repetition is penalised, and it reads badly
to the humans who scan the first two lines.

**Apple does not index it at all.** Its only job is conversion — for the minority of users
who expand it.

Both stores show only the first few lines before a "more" cut. Whatever matters has to be
above that fold. Short paragraphs, scannable lines, benefits before features.

## Icons

Not indexed, disproportionately important. The icon is what a user sees in the search grid
before they read anything, and often the only thing they see.

What holds up in practice:

- **One idea.** An icon is rendered small. A detailed illustration turns to mush.
- **Contrast against both light and dark home screens**, since you cannot control which.
- **Distinct from category convention.** If every competitor uses a blue speech bubble, a
  blue speech bubble makes you invisible.
- **No text.** It is unreadable at the rendered size, and your name is already displayed
  beside it.

## Screenshots

The highest-leverage creative asset on the page, and the one most worth testing.

**Position carries most of the effect.** The first two screenshots appear in search results
on iOS; the majority of users never swipe past the third. Rank your value propositions and
put them in that order.

What works:

- **Caption every screenshot.** A bare UI screenshot asks the viewer to work out the
  benefit. Say it.
- **Lead with the benefit, not the feature.** "Never lose a receipt" over "OCR scanning".
- **Design for the grid, not the page.** Your first frames are rendered small in a scrolling
  row. Test them at that size.
- **Localise them.** Translated captions consistently outperform English ones in non-English
  storefronts, and it is cheaper than any other localisation work.

For how to test them properly, see [screenshot and creative
testing](/blog/screenshot-and-creative-testing) — the short version is that a test needs a
control, and "we changed them and installs went up" is not a result.

## Promotional text (iOS only)

One hundred and seventy characters above the description, **updatable without a release**.
Not indexed.

That last property is the whole point: it is your only lever for a sale, a launch, a
seasonal message, or a response to something that just happened, without shipping a build
or resetting your review.

## What to change first

If you are starting from nothing, in order of expected return per hour spent:

1. **Screenshots**, especially the first two.
2. **Title**, if it currently carries no keyword.
3. **Subtitle / short description**, if it restates the title.
4. **Icon**, if it is indistinct in the category grid.
5. **iOS keyword field**, if it repeats words already in your title.
6. **Play description**, if it is keyword-stuffed or the first two lines say nothing.

## Measuring it

Store listing changes affect two different numbers and it matters which you watch:

- **Indexed field changes** (title, subtitle, keywords, Play description) move **rank**, on
  a delay of days.
- **Creative changes** (icon, screenshots, video) move **conversion rate** — impressions to
  installs — usually within a day.

If you change both at once you will not know which did what. Change one, wait, measure,
then change the other.

And separate organic from paid before drawing any conclusion. A creative test running
alongside a campaign measures the campaign. See [total installs is the wrong number to judge
ASO on](/blog/organic-vs-paid-installs).

## The short version

- iOS indexes title, subtitle and keyword field. **Not the description.**
- Play indexes title, short description and the full description.
- Never repeat a word across indexed iOS fields — Apple indexes their union.
- Icons and screenshots are not indexed and probably decide the install.
- The first two screenshots do most of the work; caption them with benefits.
- Promotional text is your only no-release lever.
- Indexed changes move rank on a delay; creative changes move conversion quickly.
