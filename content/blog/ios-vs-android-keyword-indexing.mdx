The single most common ASO mistake is treating the two stores as one problem. They index
different fields, weight them differently, and punish different things.

## What the App Store reads

Apple gives you a **100-character keyword field** that users never see. It is the highest-value
real estate in iOS ASO, and it has rules that reward precision:

- **Comma-separated, no spaces after commas.** A space costs you a character and buys nothing.
- **Do not repeat words across fields.** Apple combines your app name, subtitle and keyword field
  into one index. A word in your title does not need to be in your keywords, and putting it there
  wastes characters you cannot get back.
- **Singulars and plurals are handled** — you generally do not need both.
- **Do not include your category name or the word "app".** Apple already indexes those.

The **name** (30 characters) and **subtitle** (30 characters) are indexed and visible. Between
them and the keyword field you have 160 characters that matter.

The **description** is, for ranking purposes, close to worthless on iOS. It converts; it does not
rank.

## What Google Play reads

Google indexes the **title** (30 characters), the **short description** (80 characters) and the
**full description** (4000 characters) — the last one in full.

That inverts the strategy. On Android the long description is a genuine ranking surface, and
keyword density in it is a real lever. It is also a real trap: Google penalises keyword stuffing,
and repetition that reads badly to a human tends to read badly to the ranking system too. A
mention rate somewhere around 2–3% for your primary term is the usual guidance — enough to be
unambiguous, not enough to look automated.

There is no keyword field on Android. If you are used to iOS, that absence is where the
100 characters of intent have to go somewhere else.

## The consequence

The same listing, ported field-for-field across stores, gets it wrong in both directions:

- Ported iOS → Android: your description is thin, and you have lost your only Android ranking
  surface.
- Ported Android → iOS: your keyword field is empty or duplicates the title, and you have thrown
  away the highest-leverage field Apple gives you.

Any listing audit that scores both platforms with one rule set is telling you something false
about one of them. "Your description does not mention your primary keyword" is critical advice on
Google Play and near-irrelevant on iOS — a checker that does not know which store it is looking
at will confidently give you the wrong priority.

## Localization is not translation

One more asymmetry worth knowing: on iOS, adding a localization gives you a *new* 100-character
keyword field per storefront. Localizing into a language you do not speak, purely to claim the
extra keyword field, is a real and widely used tactic.

But an audit that measures whether fields are *filled* will score an untranslated locale
identically to a translated one. Both have a subtitle; only one is in the right language. If your
tooling reports Brazil at 72/100 while the listing is entirely in English, the score is measuring
the wrong thing — what you want to know first is whether the locale is localized at all.
