There is exactly one source of true App Store search volume available to anyone outside
Apple, and most teams already have access to it without realising what it is.

**Apple Search Ads search-term reports contain measured impressions per keyword per
storefront.** Not modelled, not estimated from autocomplete ordering — counted, by Apple,
from real searches.

Every other volume number you have seen is inferred. As covered in [our keyword research
guide](/blog/app-store-keyword-research), autocomplete ordering supports a relative index
and nothing more. ASA is the exception, and it is worth restructuring your research process
around.

## What the report actually contains

Run a Search Ads campaign — even a small one — and you can export a search-term report
showing, for each term users actually typed:

- **Impressions** — how many times your ad was eligible and shown
- **Taps** and tap-through rate
- **Conversions** and conversion rate
- **Spend** and cost per acquisition

The impressions column is the prize. It is a real measurement of demand for that term in
that storefront, in that period.

The conversion column is arguably more valuable and almost always ignored.

## Two things this tells you that nothing else can

### 1. Which terms have demand you can actually see

A term with 40,000 impressions and a term with 400 look identical in an autocomplete-derived
index if they both happen to be suggested early. ASA separates them with a number.

This immediately reprices your organic keyword list. Terms you assumed were head terms turn
out to be niche; terms you dismissed turn out to carry real volume in specific storefronts.

### 2. Which terms convert for *your* app

This is the part that changes decisions.

A term can have high volume and convert terribly for you — because the searcher wants
something adjacent to what you built. ASA shows you conversion rate per term, from real
users, before you have spent a single character of metadata on it.

**That is a live test of keyword relevance with a real budget attached.** Organic ranking
takes weeks to establish and weeks to unwind. ASA tells you in days whether a term is worth
chasing.

The rule that follows:

> **Do not spend metadata characters on a term that converts badly in ASA.**

You have 30 characters of title, 30 of subtitle, 100 of keywords. Spending them on a term
that demonstrably does not convert is the most expensive kind of mistake, because it also
costs you the ranking you would have had for something else.

## How to mine it for organic research

### Start with broad match and let Apple find terms

Set up a campaign with **broad match** or Apple's Search Match enabled and a modest budget.
Apple will show your ad against terms you did not think to bid on, and the search-term
report records every one.

This is a discovery mechanism, not just an advertising one. It surfaces the language real
users type, which is frequently not the language you would have guessed.

### Export the search-term report, not the keyword report

They are different. The keyword report shows terms **you bid on**. The search-term report
shows what users **actually typed**, including the long-tail variants that matched your
broad keyword.

The second is where the research value sits.

### Sort by impressions, then filter by conversion

Your organic priority list is roughly:

1. **High impressions, high conversion** — chase these hard, they belong in your title
2. **High impressions, low conversion** — do not spend metadata on them, whatever their
   volume
3. **Low impressions, high conversion** — long tail worth keeping; cheap to rank for
4. **Low impressions, low conversion** — ignore

Most teams look only at column one and wonder why their metadata rewrite did not move
installs.

### Segment by storefront

ASA reports per storefront. A term with real volume in the US may be dead in the UK, and
a term nobody searches in English-speaking markets may carry your entire German install
base.

If you are localising metadata, this is the data that tells you which terms to localise
*to* — rather than translating your English keywords and hoping.

## The caveats, stated plainly

**ASA impressions are ad impressions, not total searches.** You saw the searches where your
ad was eligible and won the auction. The true search count is higher, by an unknown factor
that varies with your bid, your relevance and your competition.

So use it as a **relative** measure between terms, and as an **absolute floor** — the true
volume is at least this. Do not treat it as the total.

**It only covers terms you were eligible for.** Terms far outside your category will never
appear, however popular they are.

**It costs money.** A discovery campaign is a real budget line. For most teams it is
cheaper than a year of a tool subscription that models the same numbers less accurately, but
it is not free.

**Apple's matching is fuzzy.** Search Match will show your ad against terms with only loose
relation to your keyword. Those rows are still real searches by real users, so they are
still data — just do not assume they reflect your bid.

## What to do if you do not run ASA

Then accept that your volume numbers are inferred, and design around that:

- Rely on **autocomplete ordering** for relative demand, and treat the ordering as the
  finding rather than any number derived from it.
- Weight **evidence** heavily. A term observed once is a guess; one seen across several
  independent queries and on both stores is a finding.
- Check that a **market exists** — a term with almost no competing apps has almost no
  searchers.
- Use **your own review text** as a keyword source. Users describe your app in their own
  words, and those words are what they type.

That gets you a defensible ordering. It does not get you volume, and no tool that lacks
impression data can give you volume either, whatever it prints.

## The short version

- ASA search-term reports are the only measured App Store volume available outside Apple.
- The conversion column matters more than the impressions column — it tells you which terms
  are relevant to *your* app before you commit metadata to them.
- Use the search-term report, not the keyword report.
- Segment by storefront before localising anything.
- Impressions are a floor and a relative measure, never a total.
- Without ASA, your volume figures are inferred — so lean on evidence and relevance instead
  of precision you do not have.
