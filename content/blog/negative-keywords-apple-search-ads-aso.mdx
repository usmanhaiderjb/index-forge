When running Apple Search Ads (ASA), one of the most common and expensive mistakes is paying Apple for installs **you would have received organically for free**.

If your app ranks #1 organically for your brand name or a core high-intent search term, running an automated Search Match or broad-match discovery campaign often places a sponsored ad directly above your own organic listing. Users tap the first result they see — and you are billed \$2.50 to \$6.00 for an install that was already yours.

In this guide, we show how to audit keyword cannibalization, structure negative keyword tiers, and preserve marketing margins while expanding true incremental reach.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

|                                 [Organic Rank #1]      ──► 18% Tap Organic Listing |

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

|  [User Searches "BrandName"] ──► [No Sponsored Ad Shown]                          |
|                                 [Organic Rank #1]      ──► 96% Tap Organic Listing |
|                                 Net Result: $0 spent, 96% organic capture rate     |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## The Economics of Search Cannibalization

To determine whether paid ads on rank #1 keywords are incremental or cannibalistic, you must measure the **Baseline Displacement Curve**.

When an app ranks #1 organically:
* **With No Paid Ad**: Organic position #1 captures approximately **65% to 75%** of all search clicks for non-branded terms, and **85% to 95%** for branded queries.
* **With Your Own Paid Ad at Top**: The paid ad captures **55%**, while your organic listing captures **30%**. Your total capture rises slightly from 75% to 85%, but you are paying for 55% of the traffic to gain a net increment of only 10%.

```
Incremental Cost Per Install (iCPI) = Total Paid Spend / (Total Installs - Organic Baseline)
```

If your standard Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) is \$3.00, but 80% of those users would have clicked your organic link anyway, your **true incremental CPA** is actually:

```math
\text{iCPI} = \frac{\$3.00}{1.0 - 0.80} = \$15.00
```

Paying \$15.00 per incremental user destroys unit economics for subscription and in-app purchase business models.

## The 3-Tier Negative Keyword Architecture

To prevent discovery campaigns from poaching high-ranking organic terms, implement a strict three-tier campaign hierarchy:

```
                  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │      ASA CAMPAIGN ARCHITECTURE      │
                  └─────────────────────────────────────┘
                                     │
         ┌───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
         ▼                           ▼                           ▼
┌─────────────────┐         ┌─────────────────┐         ┌─────────────────┐
│ BRAND DEFENCE   │         │ EXACT CATEGORY  │         │ DISCOVERY / EXP │
│ Target: Brand   │         │ Target: High Mid│         │ Search Match ON │
│ Negative: None  │         │ Negative: Brand │         │ Negative: Brand │
│                 │         │                 │         │ + Exact Targets │
└─────────────────┘         └─────────────────┘         └─────────────────┘
```

### 1. Brand Defense Campaign (Controlled Bidding)
* **Goal**: Defend your exact brand name against aggressive competitor conquesting.
* **Keywords**: Exact match brand tokens only (`[my brand app]`).
* **Negatives**: None.
* **Bid Strategy**: Set low-to-moderate maximum bids. If competitors bid \$5.00 on your brand, evaluate whether their presence converts. If competitor conversion on your brand is low, let them waste budget.

### 2. Exact Match Category Campaign
* **Goal**: Scale verified, high-intent category terms (e.g. `[budget planner]`, `[split bills]`).
* **Keywords**: Exact match phrases identified from keyword research.
* **Negatives**: Add all Brand terms as **Negative Exact** to ensure brand search volume never leaks into category reporting.

### 3. Discovery & Search Match Campaign
* **Goal**: Uncover new long-tail search queries through Apple's Search Match algorithm.
* **Keywords**: None (Search Match = ON, Broad Match = ON).
* **Negatives**: Add **ALL Brand terms AND ALL Category Exact keywords** as **Negative Exact**.

```
Discovery Campaign Negative List:
├── [your brand] (Negative Exact)
├── [your brand name] (Negative Exact)
├── [budget planner] (Negative Exact)
└── [expense tracker] (Negative Exact)
```

By placing every keyword you already target in Category Exact into Discovery as a Negative Exact, the discovery campaign is forced to bid *exclusively* on net-new search terms you do not currently track.

## When to Bid on Your Own Rank #1 Keywords

Is there ever a valid reason to bid on a keyword where your app ranks #1 organically?

Yes, under exactly two conditions:

| Scenario | Condition | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Aggressive Competitor Conquesting** | A well-funded competitor is running ads on your brand and capturing 25%+ of top-of-page clicks. | Running an ad pushes their sponsored slot off the first viewport, securing combined 90%+ share. |
| **Custom Product Page (CPP) Testing** | You want to test a radical new screenshot or onboarding angle without changing your primary organic listing. | Search Ads allows pairing specific CPP variants to exact search terms. |

## Tracking Organic vs Paid Attribution Accurately

The greatest obstacle in identifying cannibalization is inaccurate data reporting. If you rely solely on App Store Connect, paid and organic installs are lumped into generic totals.

To see the real split:
1. Connect Apple Search Ads and App Store Connect to a single reconciliation engine like [IndexForge Metrics Reconciliation](/features/metrics-reconciliation).
2. Subtract verified Search Ads attributed installs from total first-time downloads per storefront per day.
3. Monitor your **Organic Share of Voice** daily. When you launch a Search Ads campaign, if total installs stay flat while paid installs rise and organic installs drop by the same amount, you have proven 100% cannibalization.
