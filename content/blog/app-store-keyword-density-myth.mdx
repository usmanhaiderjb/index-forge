One of the most persistent myths in App Store Optimization is that you need to repeat a keyword multiple times across your Title, Subtitle, and Keyword field to "reinforce" its ranking power to Apple's search algorithm.

On the iOS App Store, **repeating a keyword does not increase your ranking. It simply burns characters you could have used to rank for another term.**

In this post, we look under the hood of app store search indexers: how tokenizers break strings into atomic terms, how cross-field combination works, and where token repetition actually matters (and where it actively harms you).

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        APPLE APP STORE TOKENIZER BEHAVIOR                         |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Title:     "Habit Tracker & Daily Routine"      --> [habit, tracker, daily, routine]
|  Subtitle:  "Build Habits, Track Goals"          --> [build, habits, track, goals]
|  Keywords:  "habit,tracker,routine,planner"      --> [habit, tracker, routine, planner]
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Resulting Index Set (De-duplicated):                                             |
|  [habit, tracker, daily, routine, build, habits, track, goals, planner]           |
|                                                                                   |
|  WASTED CHARACTERS:                                                               |
|  - "habit" repeated 3 times   (wasted: 10 chars)                                  |
|  - "tracker" repeated 2 times (wasted: 7 chars)                                   |
|  - "routine" repeated 2 times (wasted: 7 chars)                                   |
|  TOTAL WASTED CAPACITY: 24 characters = 3 to 4 net-new target keywords lost       |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## How Apple's Indexer Combines Tokens

Apple treats your metadata fields as a unified pool of unique tokens. When an indexing job runs, Apple's search daemon:

1. **Normalizes & Strips Punctuation**: Removes hyphens, ampersands, commas, and special symbols.
2. **Tokenizes & Stems**: Splits strings on spaces and commas into individual word tokens.
3. **De-duplicates**: Merges identical tokens across the Title, Subtitle, and Keyword field into a single mathematical set.
4. **Constructs Multi-Word Permutations**: Creates search index combinations from all tokens across all indexed fields.

```
Title Token: "habit"
Subtitle Token: "journal"
Keyword Token: "offline"

--> Automatically indexed for:
    "habit journal"
    "habit offline"
    "journal offline"
    "offline habit journal"
```

Because Apple automatically generates cross-field permutations, placing `"habit"` in your Title and `"journal"` in your Subtitle indexes your app for `"habit journal"` without needing the exact phrase written contiguously.

### The True Value Weighting of iOS Fields
While repetition adds zero value, **placement location** dictates algorithmic weight:

| Metadata Field | Max Characters | Indexing Weight | Primary Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **App Title** | 30 | **10.0x (Highest)** | Primary category seed term + strongest conversion hook |
| **Subtitle** | 30 | **5.0x (High)** | Secondary high-volume modifiers + complementary seeds |
| **Keyword Field** | 100 | **2.0x (Baseline)** | Unique long-tail words, synonyms, and combinatorial tokens |
| **In-App Purchase Name** | 30 | **1.0x (Variable)** | Exact-match transactional terms |
| **Description** | 4,000 | **0.0x (NOT INDEXED)** | Conversion only. Apple never reads description for search. |

## Where the Myth Came From: Google Play vs iOS

Why do so many ASO blogs recommend "2% to 3% keyword density"?

Because on **Google Play**, text density in the 4,000-character long description *does* influence search ranking. Google Play uses full-text indexing, meaning a single mention of a word in 4,000 characters might be treated as incidental noise, whereas 4 to 5 natural mentions across structured sections confirm topic relevance.

However, copying Google Play density tactics to iOS destroys performance:

| Strategy Aspect | Apple App Store (iOS) | Google Play (Android) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Keyword Repetition** | **Zero algorithmic benefit.** De-duplicated immediately. | **Moderate benefit** (optimal 3–5 natural mentions). |
| **Keyword Stuffing Risk** | Wasted character capacity. | **Search penalty or listing rejection** for spam. |
| **Cross-Field Permutation** | Native across Title, Subtitle, and Keyword field. | Requires contiguous multi-word phrases in text. |
| **Description Indexing** | **Ignored** by search engine. | **Fully parsed** by Google NLP. |

## 5 Practical Rules for Clean iOS Metadata

When forging your metadata in our [AI ASO Writer](/features/ai-aso-writer), follow these five rules to reclaim wasted characters:

```
[Character Audit Checklist]
├── 1. Zero Token Overlap between Title, Subtitle, and Keyword Field
├── 2. No Plural Duplication (Apple stems "workout" and "workouts" automatically)
├── 3. No Spaces after Commas in the 100-character Keyword Field ("run,jog,pace")
├── 4. No Stop Words ("the", "a", "an", "for", "with", "app") in Keyword Field
└── 5. Maximize Single-Word Modifiers in the Keyword Field
```

### 1. Strip Spaces from the Keyword Field
Apple's keyword field parser uses commas as delimiters. Writing:
`fasting, tracker, keto, timer` (30 characters)
Burns 3 characters on spaces. Writing:
`fasting,tracker,keto,timer` (27 characters)
Reclaims 3 characters — enough space to add another 3-letter keyword like `run` or `log`.

### 2. Don't Waste Space on Plurals
Apple's morphological stemmer automatically matches singular and plural forms for English and most major European languages. If you include `"tracker"`, you will automatically rank for `"trackers"`. Adding both wastes 8 characters.

### 3. Avoid Category Names and the Word "App"
Apple automatically indexes your app for its assigned primary category (e.g. `"Health & Fitness"`, `"Finance"`) and the universal token `"app"`. Putting `"free fitness app"` in your keyword field is 100% wasted space.
