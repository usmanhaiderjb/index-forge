A keyword that was ranked 87 yesterday shows nothing today. The obvious reading is that the
app fell out of the rankings. The likely reading is that it moved from position 87 to
position 103 and the tracker only looked at the first hundred results.

Those two events produce **the same blank cell** in most rank reports, and they call for
completely different responses.

## What a rank tracker actually does

There is no ranking API. Neither Apple nor Google publishes an endpoint that answers "where
does app X rank for term Y".

So every rank tracker does the same thing you would do by hand: **search the term, read down
the results, and note the position where your app appears.**

That means the measurement has an edge. The tracker reads some number of results — 100 is
common, 200 is generous — and if your app is not in that window, it has no answer.

The honest report of that situation is **"not found in the top 100"**. What most tools
record is a blank, a null, or a placeholder like 250.

## Why this matters more than it sounds

"Not found in the top 100" and "ranked 250th" are different claims:

- The first is a **limit of the measurement**. Your app is somewhere past 100; the tracker
  cannot say where.
- The second is a **measurement**. Your app is at 250.

A chart that renders both as a gap, or worse as the same number, cannot be interpreted. You
cannot tell a genuine collapse from an app that drifted one position past the edge of the
scan.

And the difference in response is total:

| Reading | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Fell from 87 to 103 | Nothing. Normal movement on a mid-tail term. |
| Fell out of the top 250 | Something broke. Investigate immediately. |

Treating the first as the second burns a week. Treating the second as the first misses a
real problem.

## The tell: does your report record its own depth?

The question to ask of any rank tracking tool:

> **How deep did you look, and did you record it with the number?**

If the answer is not in the data — not in the export, not in the API response, not on the
chart tooltip — then every gap in your history is ambiguous and always will be. You cannot
reconstruct it later, because the scan that produced the blank is gone.

A rank record should carry at minimum:

- the position, or an explicit "not found"
- **the depth that was scanned**
- the storefront
- the timestamp

Without depth, the series is uninterpretable at exactly the moments that matter most.

## Other reasons a rank vanishes

Scan depth is the commonest explanation. It is not the only one.

**The storefront changed.** A rank is per country. If a tool silently defaults to US and you
launched in the UK, you are tracking a different question.

**The term itself moved.** Search suggestions and result sets are not static. A term can
stop returning your app because the store re-interpreted the query, not because you fell.

**Your app was removed from the storefront.** Pricing changes, availability changes and
review rejections all remove an app from results entirely. This produces a blank that looks
identical to a depth problem and is considerably more urgent.

**Personalisation.** Results vary by device, language and history. A tracker measuring from
one context is one sample, not a global truth.

**The measurement failed.** The request timed out, was rate limited, or was blocked. A
tracker that records a failed fetch as "not ranked" is manufacturing data. A failed
measurement should be recorded as a failure — and should look different from a real absence
on the chart.

That last one is worth being blunt about. **A gap caused by a network error and a gap
caused by an app losing its ranking must not render identically**, because one requires
retrying and the other requires action.

## What to check, in order

When a rank goes blank:

1. **What was the scan depth?** If it is 100 and the app was at 90 yesterday, stop here. It
   is almost certainly still ranking, just past the edge.
2. **Did the fetch succeed?** A blank from a failed request is not data.
3. **Is the app still in that storefront?** Search for it by name.
4. **Did other terms move at the same time?** One term drifting is normal. Every term
   vanishing at once is either an availability problem or a tracking problem — not an ASO
   problem.
5. **Did the chart position move too?** If both fell together it is velocity. See [how app
   store rankings actually work](/blog/how-app-store-rankings-work).

Only after those five is "we lost ranking for this term" the leading explanation.

## Designing around it

If you are building or choosing rank tracking:

- **Record depth with every observation.** It costs one integer and makes the entire history
  interpretable.
- **Distinguish "not found" from "not measured".** Different states, different renderings.
- **Never substitute a placeholder number.** Writing 250 for "not found in 200" invents a
  measurement, and someone will eventually average it.
- **Scan deeper for terms you care about**, shallower for the long tail. Depth costs
  requests; spend them where a position past 100 would still be meaningful.
- **Keep the storefront and timestamp** on every row.

## The short version

- Rank tracking works by searching and reading down the results, so it has an edge.
- A blank usually means the scan ran out, not that the app fell out.
- "Not found in the top 100" and "ranked 250th" are different claims and must not render
  the same.
- A failed fetch is not an absence of ranking and must not be recorded as one.
- Depth, storefront and timestamp belong on every observation.
- Most vanishings are measurement artefacts. Check those before investigating your ASO.
