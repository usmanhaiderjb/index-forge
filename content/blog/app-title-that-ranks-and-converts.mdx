Thirty characters, doing two jobs that pull against each other.

The title is the **highest-weighted indexed field** on both stores — a term there is a
stronger ranking claim than the same term anywhere else. It is also the only text
guaranteed to be seen by every user in every context: search results, charts, the home
screen, notifications.

Optimise it purely for ranking and you get a keyword list nobody trusts. Optimise it purely
for brand and you are invisible to anyone who does not already know your name.

## Where it truncates

The number that matters is not 30. It is however many characters survive in the context
where users actually see you.

- **Product page:** the full 30 characters.
- **Search results and charts:** truncated, often around 20 characters depending on device
  width and language.
- **Home screen, under the icon:** shortest of all, frequently 10 to 12 characters before an
  ellipsis.

You cannot control which context someone meets you in. So:

> **Put the part that must survive first.**

If your title is `Pocketwise: Budget Planner`, a user scanning search results may see
`Pocketwise: Budg…` and on their home screen `Pocketwi…`. That is fine — the brand survives,
which is what home-screen text is for.

If your title is `Budget Planner & Expense Tracker — Pocketwise`, the brand never appears
anywhere except the product page, and your home screen icon is labelled `Budget P…` like
every other budgeting app.

## The shape that works

**Brand: primary keyword.**

```
Pocketwise: Budget Planner
Habitly: Habit Tracker
```

Brand first gives you recall, home-screen identity, and protection against competitors
bidding on your name. Keyword second gives you the ranking claim and tells a stranger what
the app is.

The separator matters less than people think — colon, dash and hyphen all work. Colons read
slightly cleaner and cost one character.

### When to lead with the keyword instead

One case justifies inverting it: **you have no brand recognition and no plans to build any
soon.** A new utility app with no marketing budget gets more from `Budget Planner:
Pocketwise` than from the reverse, because nobody is searching the brand.

Switch back once the brand is worth something. Title changes are cheap and re-index within
days.

## Rules that come from how indexing works

**Never repeat a word across indexed iOS fields.** Apple indexes the union of your title,
subtitle and keyword field. `Habitly: Habit Tracker` plus `habit` in your keyword field
wastes characters you could have spent on another term entirely.

**You do not need phrases.** Apple builds combinations across fields. With `Habit` in the
title and `tracker` in keywords, you are eligible for "habit tracker" without spending the
phrase. On a 30-character budget this is the difference between one term and three.

**Skip your category name.** You are already indexed for the category. `Fitness` in the
title of a fitness app is usually a wasted word.

**Skip filler.** `The`, `App`, `Free`, and `&` cost characters and add no ranking value.
`Budget Planner & Expense Tracker` becomes `Budget Planner Expense` at the same information
content and eight fewer characters.

Note the one exception: `free` is genuinely searched as a modifier. It is a real keyword,
just rarely worth a title slot.

## Rules that come from how people read

**Say what it is, not what it is like.** `Pocketwise: Money Made Simple` is a tagline. A
stranger scanning results cannot tell whether it is a budgeting app, a banking app or a
payments app.

**Avoid over-punctuation.** Titles crowded with pipes, dashes and emoji read as spam and
both stores may reject them.

**Test it small.** Render your title at search-result size and at home-screen size before
committing. Most title problems are invisible at full width and obvious at the size users
actually see.

## Play's differences

Google Play also gives 30 characters and also weights the title heavily. Two divergences:

**Play policing is stricter on promotional text in titles.** Words like `#1`, `Best`, or
`Free` in the title can trigger a policy rejection. Apple is more permissive here; do not
assume a title that passed review on iOS will pass on Play.

**Play has no keyword field**, so the title carries relatively more of the ranking load,
with the short description and full description behind it. That is an argument for spending
title characters on keywords slightly more aggressively on Play than on iOS — where the
100-character keyword field can absorb them instead.

Nothing requires the titles to match across stores, and there is a reasonable case that they
should not.

## Changing it

Title changes require a new version submission on iOS and go through review. On Play they
can be updated from Console without a release.

Either way:

- **Expect a re-indexing delay** of days. A rank dip immediately after a title change is
  normal and often recovers.
- **Change one thing.** If you change the title and the screenshots together you will not
  know which moved the number.
- **Do not thrash.** Both stores build up ranking signal for a term over time. Rotating
  keywords monthly means never accumulating any.

## The short version

- The title is the highest-weighted indexed field and the only text everyone sees.
- It truncates hard in search and harder on the home screen — front-load what must survive.
- `Brand: primary keyword` is the default; invert only when the brand is worth nothing yet.
- Never repeat a word across iOS indexed fields — Apple indexes their union.
- Target vocabulary, not phrases; Apple builds the combinations.
- Cut `the`, `app`, `&` and your category name.
- Play rejects promotional words that Apple allows. The titles need not match.
